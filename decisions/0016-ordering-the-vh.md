# 0016 — Order the VH, not the scFv; vector, cloning and vendor are separate

Date: 2026-09-23
Status: accepted
Closes: Q16 (for the working vector). Q15 still open.

## Context

`construct.build` designed the whole scFv and codon-optimised it as one
fragment. That is the right thing to synthesise only if nothing else exists
yet. In practice the light chain is a fixed part of the campaign — decision
0007 fixed it to IGKV1-39 — so synthesising it 96 times is 96 copies of the
same 321 bp, and every clone pays for it.

It also produced a fragment with no cloning features at all, which is not
orderable: it cannot go anywhere.

## Decision

The VH is what is ordered. Everything constant lives in the vector.

Four decisions are kept in four places, so each can change without the others:

| Decision | Module | Swappable by |
|---|---|---|
| what molecule | `library.construct` | `FORMATS` registry |
| where it goes | `library.vector` | `VECTORS` registry |
| how it joins | `library.cloning` | `STRATEGIES` registry |
| who makes it | `library.vendors` | `VENDORS` registry |

`library.ordering` only composes them. A different cloning strategy is an
argument, not a rewrite.

### The working vector, `pcdna-scfv-vk-v1`

    CMV — T7 — Kozak — ATG+Ig-kappa leader — [VH] — (G4S)3 — VL — His6 — stop — BGH polyA

The secretion leader is not optional. Without it the scFv stays cytoplasmic
and does not fold; it is the piece most easily forgotten when thinking about
"what is 5′ of my insert", because CMV and Kozak are the parts people name.

The light chain is IMGT `IGKV1-39*01` + the junction `STP` + `IGKJ1*01`. The
junction is recorded as its own constant because it is **not** germline-encoded
by either gene, and a reader who sees one 107-aa string labelled "IMGT" would
reasonably assume all of it was.

### Where the fusion sites go, and why not at the VH edges

Golden Gate overhangs must be identical across all 96 inserts for one vector to
accept them. VH N-termini are not identical — IGHV3-23 starts `EVQL`, IGHV1-69
starts `QVQL` — so an overhang taken from the VH itself would differ per clone
and need a different vector each time.

The sites therefore sit in the constant flanks: the last 4 nt of the leader
(`AGGA`) and the first 4 nt of the linker (`GGAG`). Each fragment carries those
few nucleotides of vector context, which is ordinary MoClo practice and leaves
the junction scarless — the overhangs are *copies* of vector sequence, not
additions to it, so the assembled plasmid reads leader + VH + linker with
nothing inserted at either seam.

`Vector.check_fusion_sites` rejects a pair that is palindromic, identical,
complementary, or differs at fewer than two positions.

### Vector sequences are constants

A vector is a physical object. Its DNA cannot be regenerated by a codon
optimiser on each run, or two runs would design fragments for two different
plasmids. The part sequences in `library.vector` were produced once by
`codon.optimize` and are now frozen; a test asserts each still translates to
the protein named beside it. Changing one means a new vector entry with a new
name, not an edit.

### Every clone's assembled reading frame is translated and checked

`cloning.verify` builds the joined ORF and translates it, checking frame,
internal stops, and that the designed protein is actually in the product. A
fragment that is correct alone and wrong once ligated is the expensive
failure — it is only visible at the join.

## Consequences

- `clone_*` columns describe the molecule (the expressed protein and the ORF
  that encodes it); `order_*` columns describe the parcel (the fragment, its
  length and GC). Conflating them is how a plate gets ordered that cannot
  express anything, so both are carried.
- Fragments are padded to a 300 bp floor for IDT eBlocks, outside the enzyme
  sites, with fixed filler that carries no Type IIs site.
- `scripts/order.py --list-options` prints every registered vector, strategy
  and vendor.
- Q15 (vendor templates) is unchanged and still open; see `docs/formats/vendors.md`.
